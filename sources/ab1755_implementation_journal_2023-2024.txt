## Open and Transparent Water Data Act — Implementation Journal
This Journal has been updated to reflect progress toward meeting legislative milestones. (1/2023)   The Open and Transparent Water Data Act was signed into law in September 2016, and in 2017 a Partner Agency Team, comprised of eight agencies, began its collaborative implementation to make water and ecological data more readily available and usable. These data help to understand California’s water management challenges and inform water initiatives across multiple sectors, such as flood preparedness, drought mitigation, water quality assessment, climate adaptation, and sustainable management of groundwater, among others. Today, we can reflect on our accomplishments and look forward to continuing progress.   This Implementation Journal offers a high-level summary of data publication strategies, accomplishments, and next steps. Clicking on the underlined text will lead you to additional useful information, and a collection of resource links is provided at the end.

## 1. The Call for Open Data
In 2016, driven by five years of historic drought, the California Legislature recognized that the State must integrate existing and future water and ecological data into an authoritative, open-access data platform to help water and resource managers operate California’s complex and multi-faceted water systems more effectively and efficiently, help water users make more informed decisions, and help scientists and engineers recognize and fill data and knowledge gaps. Making information discoverable, accessible, and usable by the public fosters entrepreneurship, innovation, and scientific discovery. Furthermore, data and tools developed using State funds should be publicly accessible.   The Open and Transparent Water Data Act directs designated State agencies to work together to create, operate, and maintain a statewide integrated water data platform, develop necessary protocols, and make available certain water and ecological data. The  Water Resilience Portfolio (2020) also recognizes the role of data in water planning and management and recommends a variety of actions in support of open data. More recently, the California Water Supply Strategy (2022) identifies the importance of improving all water management actions with better data and analytics.   Under the leadership of the Department of Water Resources (DWR), the   implementing agencies formed a Partner Agency Team, made up of eight state organizations—DWR, State Water Resources Control Board, California Department of Fish and Wildlife, California Water Quality Monitoring Council, Governor's Office of Planning and Research, California Natural Resources Agency, California Government Operations Agency, and Delta Stewardship Council. Working together, the implementing agencies met legislative requirements described in this journal. Work is ongoing to increase access to water and ecological data as they become available and to improve processes, support more use cases, and enhance user experience.

## 2. Open Water Data Available through Federation
Rather than create a new platform from scratch, the implementing agencies opted to leverage two existing, State-hosted open data portals. These portals, working together—or federated—provide access to water and ecological data, as directed by AB 1755. Leveraging the existing data portals allows good use of existing resources, enables data federation with others, and makes data available sooner and more completely.   In a federated open data system, participating open data nodes are discoverable and accessible through a connected data catalog, like an inter-library loan system, that assists data users discover what’s available.

## 2.1 Open Data Portals - CNRA Platform
The CNRA Open Data Platform  provides access to data in a transparent and useful manner. The Open Data Platform supports the CNRA’s organizations and program missions by providing an environment to publish and share useful data that can be effectively utilized by all.   As of summer 2024, the CNRA open data platform hosts over 3,100 datasets from departments within the Natural Resources Agency as well as over 17,000 datasets coming from other state and federal agencies through federation.

## 2.2 Open Data Portals - California Open Data Portal
The  California Open Data Portal , hosted by the California Government Operations Agency and the California Department of Technology, features data from many different State agencies including the Boards, Departments and Offices under the California Environmental Protection Agency and the California Health and Human Services Agency, on a wide variety of topics. The California Open Data Portal brings government closer to citizens for a shared conversation about growth and progress in our great state.   As of summer 2024, the California Open Data Portal hosts over 4,200 datasets from various State agencies, including more than 3,100 coming from the CNRA Open Data Platform through federation.

## 2.3 Making Federal Data Available
As required by the Open and Transparent Water Data Act and following the principles of data federation, the State-hosted CNRA Open Data Portal makes available to users many datasets from the five federal agencies below, including the water and ecological datasets cited in the legislation: National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) United States Bureau of Reclamation (USBR) United States Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) United States Forest Service (USFS) United States Geological Survey (USGS)   And to ensure access to critical federal databases, datasets, and data portals, the CNRA Open Data Portal also provides access to data from these other federal agencies: Bureau of Land Management (BLM)  Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) National Park Service (NPS)  United States Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) United States Census Bureau (US Census) United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) United States Environmental Protection Agency (US EPA)   These federal datasets can be found under various organizational tags including Ocean Data Partners, Water Data Partners, and individual federal publishers. The tags refer to data connections developed earlier in the federation process through targeted keyword searches on larger federal databases.

## 3. California Water Data Consortium
Founded in 2019 the  California Water Data Consortium  is an independent, nonprofit organization dedicated to supporting data-driven water management decisions in California, particularly in the face of climate change and other challenges to water resources. The Consortium works to increase access to high-quality, comprehensive, and interoperable data fostering a more resilient water future for all Californians. As a unique public-private partnership, the Consortium provides an independent space for ongoing collaboration and sustained engagement among State agencies, water agencies, industry, tribes, academia, and other nongovernmental organizations. The State plays an active role in the Consortium, as members of the Partner Agency Team, the Steering Committee, Working Group Co-Chairs, and participants in various projects. As the Consortium thrives, so does California.   In 2022, the Consortium released its Vision, Mission, Goals, and Guiding Principles document and accompanying workplan . These documents outline the priorities and actions necessary to achieve the following goals:  Advancing collaborative solutions to California’s most critical water data challenges, including piloting projects to demonstrate the value of high-quality, interoperable data for improved water management outcomes.  Cementing the Consortium as the inclusive, trusted convener in California’s water data space. Leading the California water data community in developing and propagating a broader data culture. Continuing to build the Consortium’s organizational capacity , structures, and governance.   The Consortium leads  projects  that tackle some of California’s most pressing water challenges, highlighting the diverse needs of our communities and underscoring the value of cross-sector collaboration. Additionally, the Consortium facilitates inter-agency discussions, advances data governance proposals, and emphasizes the importance of investing in data infrastructure by sharing impactful stories and amplifying key messages.

## 4. Progress toward AB 1755 Strategic Goals
To meet the legislative intent, the implementing agencies developed a strategic plan to guide activities, and continue to make outstanding progress toward the shared goals articulated in the  AB 1755 Strategic Plan  – namely, that data are: (1) sufficient, (2) accessible, (3) useful, and (4) used to inform water management in California. We are making data sufficient to support water management decision-making through on-going dataset inventory efforts. (Section 4.1) We are making data accessible through on-going publication of water and ecological datasets on the State's open data portals. (Section 4.2) We are making data more useful through testing and refinement of standards and protocols aimed at making data interoperable. (Section 4.3) We are ensuring that data will be used to promote California’s water resiliency through interagency coordination and civic engagement, such as data challenges. (Section 4.4)

## 4.1 Inventory of Water and Ecological Data
The implementing State agencies are expanding inventories of water and ecological data. These comprehensive dataset inventories include a list of datasets, identify which datasets have been published on the federated data platform, and provide additional metadata information: California Department of Fish and Wildlife dataset inventory is available through BIOS   Department of Water Resources dataset inventory dashboard State Water Resources Control Board dataset inventory dashboard

## 4.2 Publication of Water and Ecological Data
To date State Agencies have published more than 2,700 critical water and ecological datasets along with visualizations that enhance understanding the data. Over 500 of the published datasets are tagged as containing information relating to water and over 2,000 are tagged as relating to wildlife.    The State agencies have developed and are maintaining two federated open data portals used to access these data (as described in Sections 2.1 & 2.2). The portals also include metrics pages ( CA Open Data Portal , CNRA Open Data Platform ) that present information about the datasets being published, updated, and used. These metrics pages can be used to explore the current state of data publication by agency and topic as well as the use (views/downloads) of various datasets.   Additional processes are underway to digitize historic data that currently exists only in paper form. Work is ongoing to ensure State bulletins, and the data within, are available in a searchable database through the federated portals. Historic climate records, previously available only in paper format, are also being digitized and will be made available through an indexed, searchable, digital interface.

## 4.3 User Focus
At the outset of AB 1755 implementation, the State collaborated with UC Water on a report titled   Data for Water Decision Making . This report includes an initial examination of twenty diverse uses of data , or "use cases," to begin understanding the needs of various decision-makers and system users. Continuing the theme of use cases, the Open Data Portals include showcases ( CA Open Data Portal , CNRA Open Data Platform ) or demonstrations of data use from the open data portals to inform decision making.   Performance metrics and user feedback are being monitored and evaluated to guide evolution of the federated data portals. We encourage all users to provide feedback on the portals ( data.ca.gov and data.cnra.ca.gov ) and their data to better serve the people of California. Feedback received thus far has helped to improve user experience with the portal and further refine the quality or presentation of data. Additionally, users can direct questions, comments, or suggestions for improvement through the data steward links provided with each dataset. Suggestions for system improvement are being considered through project-specific work groups and public meetings hosted by the California Water Data Consortium (Section 4.4).

## 4.4 Engaging with the Community
Maximizing the value of open data requires engagement with the greater community of water data users. To that end, the implementing agencies have collaborated with non-State partners in hosting a variety of activities, with additional new and exciting opportunities on the horizon.   Partnership — The data community has embraced the concept of open water data and its many benefits. Early partnerships with academia and philanthropic organizations helped to build the foundation for a decision-driven data environment. Current partnerships with California Water Data Consortium, the Internet of Water Coalition, and California Water Quality Monitoring Council bring new and exciting dimensions to California’s water data landscape. These partnerships augment public engagement and technical expertise: The Consortium, described in Section 3 , is advancing open data in California and increase access to high quality, comprehensive, and interoperable data available to inform water decision-making throughout our state.  The Internet of Water , through use case demonstrations and peer-to-peer (P2P) networks, focuses on sharing and integrating water data at the federal-level. In Fall 2022, the Consortium became the inaugural organizational member of the newly formed Internet of Water Coalition. The California Water Quality Monitoring Council , through its theme-specific workgroups, offers opportunities data users to engage with and develop new use cases for water data. The Council also explores methods for turning data into information to communicate results effectively across diverse audiences.   Civic Engagement — Engaging communities builds connections between government programs and the constituents they serve.  Data challenges  have helped innovative users put water and ecological data to work in the fight to bring safe, clean, and affordable drinking water to every Californian. Sign up for the mailing list (select General Interests, and click the California Water Data Challenge box) or join the  slack channel to get involved.   Public Events — The AB 1755 implementing agencies continue to host informational events to increase awareness of open data and help promote its use. Please email us at AB1755@water.ca.gov to be notified of future events and other news.

## 5. Putting Data to Work
Making data sufficient, accessible, useful, and used are the primary goals of the AB 1755 Program. In so doing, we enable data to be put to work broadly, including by the State Agencies themselves, the California Water Data Consortium, and the Internet of Water Coalition, to shape a modern water data future. A few initiatives underway by each of these groups are detailed below.   State Agencies Reaching beyond its everyday use of data, the State is exploring new ways to apply water data to decision making. One such exploration is the development of a Water Accounting System, which will provide a suite of easy-to-use, online tools for use by agencies grappling with water sustainability and drought-related issues. The system will support the application of data to water management challenges, as well as coordinated and consistent water accounting among neighboring water agencies, resulting in greater understanding, better planning, and ultimately, regional resilience.   Consortium Projects   Groundwater Accounting and Data Reporting Project – The Consortium is advancing two key initiatives to support local agencies developing groundwater sustainability plans under the Sustainable Groundwater Management Act: 1) the expansion of the open-source Groundwater Accounting Platform and 2) the development of standardized groundwater data reporting formats to reduce the reporting burden on local agencies. Lidar Working Group – The Consortium celebrates the acquisition of high-quality Lidar elevation data covering all of California. This was accomplished through multi-agency collaboration and public-private partnerships with leadership from the US Geological Survey and the California Natural Resources Agency.  This group is now focused on ensuring accessibility of the various Lidar derivative products to all. The applications of these data products include hydrologic modeling, flood inundation mapping, geology and seismology, wildland fire planning and resiliency, emergency response, vegetation and carbon mapping, and more.  Telemetered Water Monitoring Project – The Consortium is developing recommendations for creating and testing telemetered water monitoring networks that are responsive to local water management needs and aligned with existing data collection practices. Urban Water Data Reporting Project  – This project aims to inform how the state can provide access to high-quality, reliable data needed for effective water management. By enhancing communication and data sharing, the project seeks to enable more informed decision-making during drought periods.     Internet of Water (IoW) Coalition California Pilot Projects Freshwater Harmful Algal Blooms (FHABs) – To enable the Water Boards to track FHABs across California and provide timely notifications of their presence, IoW collaborated with the Water Boards, The Commons, Tribal Governments, and community science organizations to develop the FHABs Monitoring Systems Data Ingestion Framework . Stream Gauges – To improve the discoverability and accessibility of streamflow and stream water quality data in California the IoW, DWR, and the Water Boards are piloting a common platform for stream sensor metadata . This platform will link stream sensor metadata to geographic locations on the stream network, making it easier to find online. The IoW is also supporting tool development to prioritize locations for additional stream sensors.

## 6. What's Next?
On the horizon are many exciting activities and products for open and transparent water data that continue to improve its accessibility and usefulness.    Additional datasets are constantly added to the already impressive data catalogs available on the State's open data portals. The implementing agencies continue to publish State-held water and ecological datasets, and efforts are underway to make additional historic records held by the State available digitally.   Federal data availability is being improved over time by refining: (1) techniques used to connect data catalogs, (2) keyword selection, and (3) location queries to ensure that federal data on the portal is relevant to water and ecological decision makers in California.   State agencies are exploring measures to move data from available to useful through improved classification and filtering of existing datasets, adoption of data publication standards, and incentivize interoperability of datasets.   An open data training curriculum is under development to enhance data literacy of State staff and standardize publication practices.   State Agencies are engaged in collaborative efforts with the Consortium to leverage State and non-state partners’ collective expertise to jointly improve California’s water data infrastructure and water management outcomes for everyone living and working in California. Visit the bottom of the Consortium homepage  to subscribe and receive announcements.

## 7. Shaping the Future
The implementing agencies invite all interested parties to make use of the State-hosted, federated open data portals, which offer an extensive and growing list of resources, datasets, and visualizations available for data users to interact with water and ecological data. Your continued use and suggestions on the data and interfaces are critical to ensuring that these tools help address California’s water management challenges and inform solutions.   Please visit the AB 1755 program webpage  with resources, announcements, and other information for more information on the multi-faceted implementation of the Open and Transparent Water Data Act, and subscribe to the AB 1755 listserv for updates by contacting us directly at  AB1755@water.ca.gov .
